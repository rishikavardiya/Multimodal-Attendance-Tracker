How the agent reads a session

Video and audio are processed on the classroom machine. Four stages turn raw signals into something an instructor can act on.

Detect and track

Computer vision finds and follows each participant across frames, live or recorded.

Measure

Head pose, movement, inactivity time and audio activity are scored per interval.

Fuse

Signals combine into one engagement trend, so a quiet but attentive room isn’t flagged as disengaged.

Reason

An agent reads the trend and writes context-aware teaching suggestions.

Suggestions tied to the moment

The agent connects a pattern to a likely cause and a concrete next step. These are the kinds of prompts an instructor sees.

Attention drop after 20 minutes

Head pose and movement fell together during a long explanation. Break the block with a short worked example.

Audio peak, high engagement

Discussion lifted the whole room. Schedule more questions or pair work around the same point next time.

Several learners idle at once

Long inactivity across one area of the room. Move closer, or check whether the slide is readable from the back.

Session analytics for each learner

After class, each learner’s session summarizes when they arrived, how long they stayed, and when their attention was highest. The goal is to help learners see their own patterns, not to rank them.

  • Arrival time and total time present
  • Attention across early, middle and late session
  • Participation moments, drawn from audio activity

Private by design

The whole system runs offline. Nothing is uploaded, no cloud service sees the classroom, and no one needs an account to be counted.

  • Processing stays on local hardware
  • Engagement is scored from behavior signals, not identity profiling
  • Instructors get trends and suggestions, not surveillance clips
